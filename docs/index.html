PRODUCT OVERVIEW

Capacitive series

Capture Contact and Force Data from Human Demonstrations

The Apex Sensing Tactile Data Acquisition Glove captures contact locations, timing and forces during human demonstrations for physical AI model training. It adds tactile measurements to human-centric datasets, complementing video and hand-motion data to characterize how people grasp and manipulate objects.

Flexible capacitive sensor arrays on the fingertips and palm capture normal and shear force data at 240 Hz. Export recordings as CSV for dataset preparation or stream data over TCP into multimodal collection pipelines. The glove runs standalone or alongside third-party hand-tracking systems.

Core capabilities and applications

Fingertip and palm coverage

7 sensing regions cover the five fingertips, central palm and pinky-side palm (hypothenar). Capture region-level contact and force distributions throughout each demonstration.

High-density tactile sensing

290 sensing points per glove, sub-2.5 mm fingertip spatial resolution and a normal-force detection threshold below 0.2 N resolve localized contact and light touch for dexterous manipulation and contact-state analysis.

Normal and shear force data

Measure normal force together with shear magnitude and direction to characterize tangential loading and contact transitions, for studying grasp stability, incipient slip and contact-state dynamics.

Glove-specific calibration

Per-glove force calibration files and one-click zeroing after donning compensate for fit-dependent baseline variation and unloaded offsets, supporting consistent measurements across users, gloves and collection sessions.

Offline export and live streaming

Export to CSV or stream via TCP/JSON for dataset archiving, offline analysis and real-time integration with your acquisition stack, data processing, model training and validation pipelines.

Lightweight wired acquisition

Under 80 g per glove, USB-powered, with total system power below 1 W. Minimal wearable burden and simple setup support repeated demonstrations and long-duration data collection.

Source discrepancy

The capability text above retains “sub-2.5 mm” from the original. The source specification table below gives fingertip spatial resolution as “< 3.5 mm”. Confirm the applicable value with the supplier.

From setup to usable data

Connect the glove to a PC over USB and start recording. The acquisition software provides live tactile heatmaps, recording, playback and export. CSV files are organized by hand and sensing region, ready for dataset curation. For live pipelines, TCP streaming sends device and channel metadata before tactile frames, making each stream self-describing for your data platform and training workflow.

INTRODUCTION

Introduction

The FULAI Tactile Glove is a high-density tactile sensing glove designed for robotic manipulation and human–object interaction research. Tactile sensor arrays on the fingertips and palm measure both normal and shear forces, capturing the full contact pressure distribution and per-zone forces whenever the hand interacts with an object.

The companion FULAI Data Acquisition Software provides real-time pressure heatmaps and zone force plots, session recording and playback, CSV export, and real-time data streaming over TCP in JSON Lines format. It supports single-hand and dual-hand acquisition, with built-in force calibration import and tactile zeroing, so glove data can feed directly into your algorithms and robotic systems.

Tactile acquisition runs standalone and does not require a third-party hand tracking system. To capture hand pose as well, add the optional hand tracking hardware and enable Pose Data in the software (see Appendix A).

This manual covers FULAI Data Acquisition Software V1.1.6 and FULAI Tactile Glove V3.0 to V3.4. The software requires 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 and a graphics card that supports OpenGL 3.3 or later.

Key features

Feature Description
Real-time display Live pressure heatmap of the hand and force–time plots for each sensing zone.
Recording and playback One-click recording with playback at 0.5×, 1.0×, 1.5×, or 2.0×. There is no limit on recording duration or number of recordings; storage is limited only by available disk space. Recording stops automatically if the device disconnects or the disk becomes full (see Chapter 10).
CSV export Export recordings to CSV, including the left and right Glove IDs, calibration status, zone forces, and per-taxel pressure.
TCP streaming Stream data in real time over TCP in JSON Lines format (default 127.0.0.1:9870) for use in other applications.
Calibration and zeroing Import the force calibration file for each glove. Tare the tactile sensors with one click after putting on or reconnecting the glove.

Test conditions

  • Trigger threshold: The palm is tested with a 10 mm diameter flat-ended rigid cylindrical indenter. All other zones are tested with a 15 mm diameter indenter.
  • Normal force accuracy: Same indenters as above. Accuracy = |mean measured value − applied force| / full scale × 100%, taken at the point of maximum error within the range.
  • Shear force accuracy: Same indenters as above, with shear applied along the sensing surface. Accuracy = |mean measured value − applied shear force| / full scale × 100%, taken at the point of maximum error.
  • Point-to-point uniformity: A 70% FS load is applied to all fingertips and recorded once values are stable. Coefficient of variation = standard deviation / mean × 100%.
  • Unit-to-unit consistency: 10 randomly selected units, 70% FS load, recorded once values are stable. Coefficient of variation = standard deviation / mean × 100%.
  • Signal drift, constant load: 15 mm indenter, 10% FS load, signal monitored for 8 hours. Drift = |initial output − final output| / (full-scale output × log₁₀(time span in seconds)) × 100%.
  • Signal drift, temperature: 8 mm indenter, 280 g static load, temperature raised from 30 °C to 50 °C. Temperature coefficient = (output at high temperature − output at reference temperature) / (full-scale output × temperature difference) × 100%.
  • Cycling stability: 15 mm indenter, 50% FS load, signal compared before and after 1,000 load cycles. Drift = (maximum output − minimum output) / full-scale output × 100%.
Note

FS = full scale. Actual performance depends on hand shape, glove fit, test method, and software settings.

CHAPTER 03

Quick start

Applies to the V1.1.6 user manual

Software V1.1.6 · FULAI Tactile Glove V3.0–V3.4 · Windows 10 / 11 (64-bit). For capacitive hardware V1.0, consult its product overview and confirm setup details with the supplier.

Follow these steps to connect the glove, zero the sensors, and record and export tactile data.

  1. Connect the Tactile Controller. Connect the Tactile Controller to your computer via USB. In Device Manager, check that a new COM port appears.
  2. Start the software. Double-click Fulai_Tactile_Glove_Production.exe. Check that the window title reads Tactile Data Acquisition and the status bar shows software version V1.1.6.
  3. Configure the data connection. Open Settings → Data Connection and select Tactile Data. Select the Glove Version shown on the glove label and the correct COM port. For tactile-only recording, leave Pose Data cleared.
  4. Enter Glove IDs and import calibration. Enter the Left Glove ID and Right Glove ID, then import the force calibration file for each glove. The calibration status changes to Imported.
  5. Connect. Click Connect. The button changes to Disconnect, and Tactile Status in the status bar shows Connected.
  6. Zero the sensors. Open your hand naturally without touching anything, then click Tactile Zero in the title bar. The icon briefly turns green.
  7. Check the response. Press lightly on an object. The pressure heatmap and zone force plots should respond immediately.
  8. Record. Click Start Recording, perform the task, then click Stop Recording. The recording status changes to Completed.
  9. Export. Export the recording to CSV. Check that the file opens correctly in Excel as UTF-8 and contains the Elapsed Time (s) column.
Tip

Force calibration is performed at the factory. You only need to re-import the calibration file when you switch to a different computer or glove. For day-to-day use, simply run Tactile Zero each time you don the glove, reconnect it, or before an important recording.

CHAPTER 04

Hardware setup

4.1 Package Contents

  • Tactile gloves (left, right, or as ordered)
  • Tactile Controller with USB cable
  • Force calibration file for each glove, typically named <Device Code>_left.json / <Device Code>_right.json
  • FULAI Data Acquisition Software installer or extracted folder

4.2 Connecting the Hardware

Each glove connects to your computer through a Tactile Controller. The controller connects to the computer via USB and to the glove via its glove cable. For single-hand acquisition, connect only the glove you intend to use.

  1. Install FULAI Data Acquisition Software V1.1.6 on your computer (Windows 10 / 11).
  2. Connect the Tactile Controller via USB. It is identified as VID:PID 0483:5740.
  3. Open Device Manager → Ports (COM & LPT) and check that a new COM port appears. The default Tactile Baud Rate in the software is 2000000.
  4. Under Hand Selection, select Left or Right for single-hand recording. For dual-hand recording, select Both and assign a different COM port to each hand.
  5. Select the Glove Version that matches the wrist label, enter the label's Device Code in the matching Left Glove ID or Right Glove ID field, and import the force calibration file.

The table below shows how the wrist label fields correspond to the software settings. Each glove has a unique Device Code. Make sure the left and right Glove IDs are not swapped, and do not enter the Model in a Glove ID field.

Setting Description
Single-hand mode Select Left or Right under Hand Selection. Configure and connect only that glove.
Dual-hand mode Each glove uses its own COM port. Select the port for each hand separately.
Right-hand auto-detect Auto-detect is enabled for the right-hand port by default. To use only the left hand, select Left and configure the left-hand COM port.
Left / Right Glove ID The Device Code on the wrist label. Enter each glove's ID separately.
Glove Version The Model on the wrist label. For example, if the label reads FulaiTactileGlove_V3.1, select V3.1.
Force calibration The calibration file must match the glove and its version. Import a separate file for each hand.

CHAPTER 05

Installation & startup

5.1 System Requirements

  • Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit
  • Dedicated graphics or recent integrated graphics with OpenGL 3.3 support
  • A free USB port. For dual-hand recording, the computer must be able to enumerate two serial ports at the same time.

5.2 Installation

  1. Get the FULAI Data Acquisition Software package from FULAI or the designated download channel.
  2. Extract it to a local folder, for example D:\FULAI\Gloves. Do not run the software from inside the archive.
  3. Administrator rights are usually not required. If your antivirus software blocks the program, add the folder to its allow list and try again.
  4. If no COM port appears in Device Manager, install the USB-to-serial driver for the controller (typically CH340, CP210x, or STM32 VCP, depending on your device).

5.3 Starting the Software

Open the installation folder and double-click Fulai_Tactile_Glove_Production.exe. You can also run RUN_APP.cmd.

When only tactile data is enabled, the title bar shows Tactile Data Acquisition and the window title is FULAI Tactile Data Acquisition. The status bar shows the software version (V1.1.6).

Note

Only one instance of the software can run at a time. If you start it again, a message appears and the open window is brought to the front. This prevents two processes from competing for the same serial port.

5.4 Finding the COM Port

  1. Plug in the Tactile Controller.
  2. Right-click Start → Device Manager, then expand Ports (COM & LPT).
  3. Note the new COM port number, for example COM24.
  4. In the software, go to Settings → Data Connection, click the refresh button next to the port list, and select that COM port.
Tip

To confirm which port belongs to the controller, unplug it. Its COM port disappears, and appears again when you plug it back in.

5.5 Troubleshooting a Blank 3D Viewport (OpenGL)

Rendering the 3D hand model requires OpenGL 3.3 or later. Remote desktop sessions, virtual machines, and systems without GPU hardware acceleration typically cannot provide OpenGL 3.3, so the viewport may appear black. If this happens, run the software directly on a local computer and make sure your graphics driver is up to date. If the issue persists, click Copy Diagnostics and send the diagnostic report, along with your software version, to FULAI support.

CHAPTER 07

Calibration & zeroing

Force calibration, calibration import, and tactile zeroing serve different purposes at different stages.

Function Purpose Performed by When
Force Calibration Maps raw sensor ADC values to actual force. FULAI (usually) At the factory. If needed, a technician or customer can recalibrate with FULAI guidance.
Import (Left / Right Force Calibration) Loads a glove's calibration parameters into the software. User or system integrator After changing computers, reinstalling the software, or changing gloves.
Tactile Zero Removes the sensor offset in the current unloaded state. User After putting on or reconnecting the glove, and before each recording session.

7.1 Importing Force Calibration

Each glove ships with its own force calibration files, typically named <Device Code>_left.json and <Device Code>_right.json. Before importing, enter the matching Left Glove ID or Right Glove ID; otherwise the software asks you to enter the Glove ID first.

Import a separate file for each hand. The file's channel count must match the selected Glove Version, or the import fails.

If no glove-specific file is imported, the software uses its built-in default calibration: the status bar shows Default and the settings page shows Not Imported. The default calibration still outputs force and pressure, but the values are not specific to your glove. Import the glove's own calibration file before any quantitative recording.

After a successful import, the status changes to Imported and the file is saved locally to:

LOCAL PATH
%APPDATA%\FULAI\Fulai_Tactile_Glove\tactile_calibration\<Glove ID>\<Version>\left.json
%APPDATA%\FULAI\Fulai_Tactile_Glove\tactile_calibration\<Glove ID>\<Version>\right.json

7.2 Tactile Taring

Tactile Taring is the first icon in the title bar (see Figure 3).

  1. Check that Tactile Status in the status bar shows Connected.
  2. Open your hand naturally. Do not press on any object or make a fist.
  3. Click Tactile Taring (circular-arrow icon) in the title bar. When the icon briefly turns green, taring is complete.
Check

With no load, the heatmap is almost blank. When you press lightly, the heatmap and zone force plots rise immediately.

Note

Zeroing is not available in Demo Mode. Zero again after reconnecting the controller, changing users, or putting the glove back on.

Main window & controls

The main window consists of four areas: the tactile view, the zone force plots, the Recording panel, and the status bar. Zone force plots are displayed separately for each hand and each sensing zone, in Newtons (N). CHAPTER 09

Reading the heatmap

The pressure heatmap color-codes the pressure at each taxel, with warmer colors indicating higher pressure. By default, taxels below 5 kPa are not colored, and taxels at or above 125 kPa are shown at full saturation. These thresholds affect only the visualization; they do not alter the recorded data.

The zone force plots show the total normal force in each sensing zone, in newtons (N), and are not clipped by the heatmap’s upper threshold. Both the glove-specific calibration and the built-in default calibration produce pressure values, but only the glove-specific calibration should be used for quantitative analysis.

Symptom Solution
No heatmap when pressing Verify that the device is connected and data is updating. If you need to re-zero, remove all external load first, run Tactile Zero with the glove unloaded, then reapply load and check the response. If the data changes but the heatmap appears faint, make sure Show Pressure Layer is enabled and check the heatmap thresholds.
High pressure with no external force Hold your hand open without touching anything. Inspect the glove for localized pressure points, fabric wrinkles, or mounting preload, and confirm that the selected glove version matches the calibration file. Then run Tactile Zero again and check both the numerical values and the heatmap. If the issue persists, suspend quantitative data acquisition and contact FULAI support.

CHAPTER 11

CSV data format

CSV files are UTF-8 encoded with a byte order mark (BOM). The first row contains the column names, and each following row is one sample. Per-point columns are named <Hand>_<Zone>_<Index>(kPa), for example Left_Thumb_01(kPa). Hardware channel numbers (A1, A2, …) are not used.

Columns fall into three groups: recording information, zone force and shear data, and per-point pressure data. Files normally contain columns for both hands. If only one glove is connected, the other hand's values are 0. The CSV does not contain joint angle data.

11.1 Recording Information Columns

Column Description Unit / values Example

Source Live or demo data Live / Demo Live
Recorded Data Data types in this recording — Tactile Data Acquisition
Version Glove version — V3.1
Left Glove ID Left Glove ID from the settings — G-20260917-Left
Right Glove ID Right Glove ID from the settings — G-20260909-Right
Force Calibration Calibration source Imported / Default Imported
Hand Hand selection Left / Right / Both Left
Start Time Date and time of the sample yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss 2026-09-11 15:19:33
Sample Index Frame number Starts at 0 0
Elapsed Time (s) Time since recording started s 1.2333
Peak Pressure (kPa) Highest pressure in the frame kPa 22.000
Active Sensors Number of sensing points above 5 kPa in this sample Count 12

Active Sensors uses a fixed 5 kPa threshold that does not change with the heatmap display threshold. When you import an existing CSV, its Active Sensors values are kept; if they are missing, they are calculated with this threshold. Recordings from earlier versions are played back with their original counts.

11.2 Zone Force Columns

Each hand has 7 sensing zones, in this order: thumb, index, middle, ring, little finger, palm, and hypothenar, plus an optional thenar zone between the palm and hypothenar (not fitted by default). Each zone has 5 columns. Force is in N; shear magnitude and its X and Y components are relative values; shear direction is in degrees.

Column (example: left thumb) Description Unit
Left_Thumb_Force(N) Total normal force in the zone N
Left_Thumb_Shear(rel) Shear magnitude Relative
Left_Thumb_ShearDirection(deg) Shear direction Degrees
Left_Thumb_ShearX(rel) Shear X component Relative
Left_Thumb_ShearY(rel) Shear Y component Relative

11.3 Per-Point Columns

Per-point columns follow the zone force columns and are in kPa. The software converts the tared ADC value of each point to force (N) using the force calibration, then to pressure (kPa) using the area of the point. Without a glove-specific file, the built-in default calibration is used and the units are the same. If the calibration appears abnormal, stop quantitative recording, check the file and glove version, and import it again.

Points are numbered from 01 within each zone. For example, on V3.2 / V3.3 / V3.4 gloves, the left thumb columns are Left_Thumb_01(kPa) to Left_Thumb_82(kPa); right-hand columns start with Right. Each hand is numbered separately and written in zone order.

On V3.2 / V3.3 / V3.4 gloves, the thumb has 90 hardware channels, of which the software exports 82 valid points. CSV files you import must contain an Elapsed Time (s) or elapsed_s column.

CHAPTER 12

TCP / JSON streaming

To stream tactile data to another application in real time, enable TCP output under Settings → Data Broadcast.

Item Description
Role The FULAI software is the TCP server; your application connects as a client.
Default state Enabled. Disable it if you do not need to stream data.
Default address / port 127.0.0.1:9870
Encoding UTF-8
Format JSON Lines: one JSON object per line, terminated by a newline
Protocol ID tactile-glove-jsonl/1
Send interval Pending data is sent in batches approximately every 8 ms. Actual output depends on device updates; each batch can contain one frame per hand.
Slow clients If the client reads more slowly than data is sent, only the latest frame is kept. Missed frames are not resent.
Reconnection Clients can reconnect at any time. On each new connection, the server first sends a meta message.

12.1 The meta Message

When a client connects, the server first sends a message with type set to meta. It describes the protocol, glove version, channel count, calibration source, and sensing zones. The example below shows selected fields; refer to the received message for the complete set.

JSON
{
  "type": "meta",
  "protocol": "tactile-glove-jsonl/1",
  "channel_count": 401,
  "glove_version": "v3.1",
  "glove_id": "G-202609-099",
  "calibration_source": "imported",
  "zones": [
    { "name": "Thumb", "address": 1, "offset": 0, "count": 50 }
  ]
}

12.2 The frame Message

After the meta message, the server sends one frame message per hand for each data update.

Field Description
type Always frame.
seq Incrementing sequence number.
timestamp Unix time in seconds, with three decimal places.
hand left or right.
glove_id Glove ID. Left-hand frames carry the left Glove ID; right-hand frames carry the right Glove ID.
calibration_source imported: glove-specific calibration. bundled: built-in default calibration. Check values_unit for the actual unit.
values 1D array in hardware channel order. In N with a valid force calibration; otherwise tared ADC values. See values_unit. Its length equals channel_count in the meta message.
zones[].sum Sum of all channels in the zone. See zone_value_unit.
zones[].shear Relative shear value.
pose Valid only when pose data is enabled and available for this hand. Ignore it for tactile-only use.
values_unit / zone_value_unit N: valid force calibration. tared_adc: tared ADC values. Determine the unit from these fields, not from the calibration source.
Important

With a valid force calibration, TCP values are point forces (N) in hardware channel order, while CSV per-point columns are pressures (kPa) in valid-point order. When the calibration source is bundled, the built-in default calibration is used. Always read the unit fields in your client; do not infer the unit from the calibration source alone.

CHAPTER 15

Troubleshooting

"Program is already running" message

Switch to the open window. You do not need to start the software again.

Software does not start or crashes

Check that the package was fully extracted. Add the folder to your antivirus allow list. Do not run the software from inside the archive.

Black 3D view

Update the graphics driver. Remote desktop sessions, virtual machines, and environments without hardware acceleration often cannot provide OpenGL 3.3; run the software on a local computer. If it still fails, click Copy Diagnostics (see Section 5.5).

No COM port

Try another USB port, install the serial driver, and click refresh in the software.

Port in use

Close other programs using the COM port, and make sure only one instance of the software is running.

Tactile disconnected or not detected

Reconnect the USB cable, refresh the COM port list, and check that the baud rate is 2000000.

Left / right port error

Do not select the same COM port for both hands. Assign a specific port to each hand.

No heatmap when pressing

Check that the device is connected and data is updating. To zero again, first remove all external load, zero in the unloaded state, then apply load again and check the response. If the data changes but the heatmap is faint, check that the pressure layer is on and review the heatmap thresholds.

High pressure with no external force

Open your hand without touching anything. Check the glove for local pressure points, wrinkles, or assembly preload, and confirm that the glove version and calibration file match. Then zero again and check both the values and the heatmap. If the problem persists, stop quantitative recording and contact FULAI support. Raising the display threshold only hides the problem; it does not remove abnormal pressure data.

Left and right heatmaps are swapped

Check the COM port and calibration file assigned to each hand. Confirm them against the wrist labels and import the correct files.

Force values are clearly wrong

Make sure the glove's own force calibration file is imported and the glove version matches. Without it, force and pressure data are not suitable for quantitative analysis.

Lag or stuttering

Turn off unnecessary display effects, split long sessions into shorter recordings, and make sure your TCP client reads data promptly.

Garbled characters in the CSV

The file is UTF-8 with BOM. In Excel, open it with Data → From Text/CSV and select UTF-8.

Can data from different gloves get mixed up?

No. Each glove has its own Glove ID and calibration file. Make sure you select the correct COM port for each glove.

Glove battery empty or wireless disconnection

Tactile data is transmitted through the wired USB controller. Check the controller's power supply, the USB connection, and the cables.

Recording tactile data only

Clear Pose Data and leave Tactile Data selected.

Using one hand only

Connect only the glove you need and set the other hand's port to Not Connected.

APPENDIX A

Hand pose tracking

This appendix applies only if you use the optional hand pose tracking hardware to capture hand pose and joint angles. For tactile-only recording, you can skip it.

The software includes the pose SDK, so it can receive glove pose data directly through the wireless receiver without a third-party pose application. Input over UDP from a third-party pose application is also supported.

Connection mode Requirements Configuration
Wireless Receiver Pose gloves and the matching USB wireless receiver Select Wireless Receiver. The software finds and connects to the receiver automatically; no COM port, IP address, or port settings are needed.
UDP A third-party pose application with broadcasting enabled Enter the same IP address, joint angle port, and joint position port as the sender.

A.1 Connecting via the Wireless Receiver

  1. Plug the wireless receiver into a USB port on your computer and turn on the pose gloves. Close any third-party pose application or serial tool that might be using the receiver.
  2. Go to Settings → Data Connection, select Pose Data, set Connection Mode to Wireless Receiver, and click Connect. The software finds the receiver automatically; you do not need to select a COM port.
  3. Follow the connection check window to complete the power-on check, the preparation pose, and the fit check. When the gloves fit well and the sensors are in place, click I'm Wearing the Gloves.
  4. On first use, when no calibration profile is loaded, or when prompted, run R Quick Calibration. The countdown during the preparation pose is only a guide; it does not mean that hardware calibration has succeeded.
  5. Check that pose data is received continuously. Then open your hand, make a fist, and touch your thumb to each finger while watching the 3D model. If only Receiver Connected is shown, also check that the gloves are switched on and sending valid pose data.
  6. To record tactile data at the same time, also select Tactile Data, then connect the tactile gloves, import the force calibration, and zero the sensors (see Chapters 6 and 7).
Note

Tactile zeroing and pose calibration are not interchangeable. Tactile Zero removes the unloaded tactile offset; pose calibration sets the hand pose reference.

A.2 Pose Calibration

Put on the gloves correctly before calibrating. If a recording is in progress, stop and save it first. After calibrating, check that the 3D model follows your hand before you start recording.

Calibration When to use Completion
R Quick Calibration First use, no calibration profile, or neutral pose drift Hold the guided pose steady until the software reports success.
Gesture Calibration New user, or inaccurate open/close range or thumb-to-finger contact Complete all 7 poses and the pinch check. The result is saved automatically.
Hardware Calibration Abnormal sensor calibration status, or when requested by FULAI support Follow the on-screen guide. Success is confirmed by the status check.

R Quick Calibration

  1. Click R Quick Calibration or press R, and check which hand the window shows. With one glove connected, position that hand. With both gloves online, position both hands as prompted.
  2. Keep your wrist straight, your fingers straight and together, and your thumb relaxed and slightly out to the side. You can rest your elbow on the table. Hold the pose shown in the window.
  3. Click Ready, Start R and hold still for about 3 seconds, until the software reports success. If calibration fails, correct the pose according to the message and try again.
  4. Relax your hand and check that the model follows when you open your hand and make a fist. If opening, closing, or pinching is still inaccurate, continue with Gesture Calibration.

Gesture Calibration

Click Gesture Calibration to open the calibration window, click Start Calibration, and follow the image and instructions for each step:

  1. Straight hand (neutral)
  2. Open hand (range of motion)
  3. Fist (range of motion)
  4. Pinch thumb and index finger
  5. Pinch thumb and middle finger
  6. Pinch thumb and ring finger
  7. Pinch thumb and little finger

For each pinch, touch the pad of your thumb to the pad of the indicated finger. Open your fingers when prompted, then pinch again to verify. Wait for the software to confirm each step before moving on; do not rush through the poses.

The result is saved automatically when calibration succeeds. If the software reports a pose conflict, sampling timeout, or data interruption, correct it as prompted and try again. The time required depends on how quickly each pose is completed.

A.3 Hardware Calibration

Hardware calibration is separate from R Quick Calibration and Gesture Calibration, which are done while wearing the gloves. To start it, open the magnetic calibration panel in the connection check window, then follow the Glove Hardware Calibration window.

  1. Take off the gloves and prepare the calibration box. Keep away from strongly magnetic objects.
  2. Click Start Hardware Calibration.
  3. Perform the steps in the order shown: restart the device, place it as instructed, and perform the figure-eight and six-face motions.
  4. When calibration succeeds, put the gloves back on and run R Quick Calibration.
Note

On-screen animations and countdowns are only a guide. Calibration success is confirmed by the status check.

A.4 Connecting via UDP

  1. Start the third-party pose application, connect the pose device, and enable data broadcasting.
  2. In the FULAI software, go to Settings → Data Connection, select Pose Data, and set Connection Mode to UDP.
  3. Enter the IP address and ports configured on the sender. When both run on the same computer, the defaults are IP address 127.0.0.1, joint angle port 9011, and joint position port 9013. If you change them on the sender, change them here as well.
  4. Click Connect. Check that pose data updates continuously and that the 3D model follows your hand.
Note

Calibrate third-party devices according to the manufacturer's instructions. The pose calibration options available in this software depend on the current connection mode.

A.5 Troubleshooting

Receiver not found

Check the USB connection, close any program using the receiver, and connect again.

Receiver online, but pose does not update

Check that the gloves are on and charged. If raw data is received but the software reports no pose or no calibration profile, run R Quick Calibration as prompted.

Model is offset or pinching is inaccurate

Check the glove fit. Run R Quick Calibration first, then Gesture Calibration if needed.

Data interrupted during calibration

Restore the connection and try again as prompted. The end of a countdown does not mean that calibration succeeded.

No joint angles in the CSV

CSV export contains tactile data only and has no joint angle columns. Enabling pose data does not change the export format.
